# ADR-002: Fixed-roof flat-ceiling space analysis

Status: implemented for the bounded R3 preview.

The architectural source and the R2 roof solve remain authoritative. R3 reads the already generated roof and the room boundary. It never edits roof vertices, pitch, eave elevation, bearing elevation, support references, or wall heights. A proposed ceiling can be accepted separately by the editor as a room edit in its existing transaction system.

The geometry kernel triangulates each simple room boundary and each roof facet, then intersects their triangles by convex polygon clipping. Every resulting polygon has one affine roof-height function. Projected intersection area must cover the room, and positive-area overlap is independently rejected. Computation uses an origin at the first room boundary point and floor-relative heights to avoid cancellation at large drawing coordinates. Physical units determine tolerance; there is no analysis of unassigned unitless geometry.

Roof and ceiling surfaces have zero mathematical thickness by default. No assembly declaration is required to calculate, apply or section the space. An absent assembly supplies an empty optional-offset list and no reservation boxes without creating source objects. The historical `declared` report field is retained for compatibility and now contains the complete modeled envelope; `assumptions.analysisMode` distinguishes `mathematical-zero-thickness` from `declared-thickness`, and `roofAssemblyDeclared` records whether an optional assembly actually exists. Each normal roof-layer thickness contributes `thickness * sqrt(1 + slopeX^2 + slopeY^2)` to the vertical deduction. The separate, optional room ceiling-finish thickness is a vertical reservation above the ceiling, deducted after the roof surface or reservation lower face is selected. Existing explicit values remain effective, including a room finish offset with no roof assembly.

Framing and service reservations are declared, axis-aligned boxes in building-local XYZ. Their XY rectangles partition the clipped room cells. The lower envelope of the roof underside and intersecting reservation lower faces determines the maximum finished-ceiling elevation. A reservation wholly below the floor or above the local roof envelope contributes no constraint. A box crossing the floor makes a full-room flat ceiling infeasible. No unmodeled truss or connection shape is invented.

Integrals of affine functions over clipped polygons give volumes. Another half-plane clip gives the exact area meeting a headroom threshold. Maximal flat elevation is the minimum of the affine envelope at every clipped polygon vertex, including newly created room/roof and reservation intersections. It is not a search over original roof vertices or a point grid.

Current and target ceilings each report feasibility, requested flat volume, and—only if feasible—usable flat volume and headroom area. Infeasible proposals have null usable quantities. Their distinctly named `clippedEnvelopeVolume` and `clippedEnvelopeHeadroomArea` remain geometric diagnostics, not a claim that a rejected flat ceiling can occupy that irregular volume. The maximum flat candidate likewise has a null usable volume if it cannot meet the declared minimum height. Recovery is conditional on a feasible maximum flat candidate.

Sections intersect the same convex arrangement with a building-local X or Y line. Facet and reservation event coordinates produce exact affine segments, including vertical steps at reservation boundaries. The result includes segmented floor/current/target/maximum-flat profiles, elevation and clear-height dimensions, and labels. Concave room cuts retain separate occupied intervals instead of drawing across an outside recess.

Bounds: at most 256 room vertices, 1,024 roof triangles, 128 reservations, 4,096 arrangement cells, and 2,000,000 charged intersection operations per room. The editor also caps a document at 64 room studies and 4,000,000 charged operations, passing each study the remaining allowance and reporting unprocessed rooms as unsupported. Failed attempts retain consumed-work counts. Unsupported inputs, incomplete/overlapping coverage, nonplanar facets, invalid polygons, or exceeded work bounds return explicit unsupported status and null declared quantities. Floating predicates and clipping are evaluated within a physical tolerance, not with an exact-arithmetic guarantee. Zero-area contacts are excluded from occupied area and volume.

This kernel supports flat ceiling reports under a fixed, valid roof; the rectangular tray consumer uses the same mathematical arrangement. It does not generate vaulted ceilings, stairs or access routes, reserve undeclared truss members, certify framing, size lumber/connectors, or alter roof geometry. Geometric calculations and acceptance do not wait for construction details. `verified` is always null and framing verification remains `unverified`. Both prefabricated-truss design and manufacturing-complexity surcharges remain zero.

Analytical tests cover rectangle and H footprints, roof/room intersection limits, partial and overlapping reservation boxes, normal/vertical thickness separation, infeasible targets and floor conflicts, missing/overlapping coverage, physical units, large-coordinate translations, input nonmutation, and disjoint section intervals. The installed application's behavior is verified separately by the desktop harness and physical desktop observations.

The `useZeroThickness` editor operation sets only the selected room finish offset and the selected roof layer list to zero. Existing reservation boxes, accepted ceiling elevations and profiles, room/bearing geometry, and other roof declarations are preserved. A shared assembly is copied before its layer list is cleared. The room/roof association and optional source revision/hash are checked against current generated analysis. The operation is one existing grouped Undo/Redo transaction and one source revision. Optional thickness can be declared again later without changing the authoritative roof surface. Tray return thickness and material volume are exactly zero; their geometric surface areas remain measurable.
